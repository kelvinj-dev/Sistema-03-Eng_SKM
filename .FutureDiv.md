### ROLE & EXPERTISE
Act as a Principal Marine Systems & Functional Safety Engineer with deep expertise in offshore lifting appliances, control theory, and formal verification methods (deontic logic).

### OBJECTIVE
Define the applicable international standards, control theories, and formal safety rules for designing and automating a **crane winch on a marine/offshore installation**.

### CONTEXT & SCOPE
The system operates in a harsh offshore marine environment subject to dynamic wave loads (sea states), vessel heave/roll, and strict classification society regulations. I am exploring how **deontic safety** (formalizing obligations, permissions, and prohibitions) maps onto traditional marine functional safety and winch automation.

### REQUIRED OUTPUT STRUCTURE
Organize your response into the following four sections using clear markdown tables and bullet points:

1. **Mechanical & Structural Standards:** Provide a comparison table covering key maritime and offshore standards (e.g., API, DNV, ISO, EN, ABS), detailing the issuing body and exact scope for winch drums, wire ropes, brakes, and dynamic loads.
2. **Automation & Functional Safety Standards:** List the mandatory electrical, marine automation, and functional safety standards (e.g., IEC 61508, ISO 13849-1, IACS), specifying typical SIL/PL requirements.
3. **Applicable Control Theories:** Detail the specific closed-loop and predictive control methodologies used in marine winches (e.g., Active Heave Compensation, Constant Tension, Anti-Sway, and Fail-Safe fault tolerance).
4. **Deontic Safety Integration:** Map formal deontic principles (Obligations vs. Prohibitions) directly to the winch's Safety Instrumented Functions (SIFs) and hard interlocks.

### CONSTRAINTS & TONE
* Use precise engineering terminology without unnecessary introductory filler.
* Keep explanations concise, actionable, and strictly grounded in established maritime and control engineering practices.
* If a standard depends on specific variables (such as drive type or vessel class), briefly note the distinction rather than guessing.

Why This Works (Best Practices Breakdown)

• Structured Markdown Delimiters (###): Anthropic and Hugging Face heavily validate using clear structural tags (like XML tags or Markdown headers) to separate instructions, context, and constraints. This prevents the model from blending background context with formatting rules.
• Domain-Specific Persona (ROLE): Google's prompting guides emphasize assigning a specific, senior-level persona to anchor the model's vocabulary, depth, and risk-awareness (e.g., immediately prioritizing SIL/PL ratings and Dynamic Amplification Factors).
• Explicit Format & Granularity (REQUIRED OUTPUT STRUCTURE): LLMs perform significantly better when told how to organize data (e.g., requesting a table for comparative standards vs. bullets for control theories) rather than leaving layout choices open-ended.
• Guardrails Against Hallucination (CONSTRAINTS): Instructing the model to note dependencies rather than guess aligns with Anthropic's and Google's grounding best practices, reducing generic or fabricated compliance claims.
• Bridging Concepts (CONTEXT): Instead of asking about "deontic safety" in a vacuum—which defaults to abstract AI philosophy—anchoring it to a physical domain ("marine crane winch") forces the model to synthesize formal logic directly into engineering interlocks.
